Part 1

Where to read

No single outlet is neutral. The trick is to read a straight news source first, then see how the same story is told from the left and the right. Lean labels below are rough and widely used guides, not verdicts.

Leans leftCentre / straight newsLeans right

Start here: straight news

Wire services and broadcasters with strict impartiality rules. Dry on purpose, which is the point.

  • BBC NewsCENTREFREE

    UK public broadcaster with a legal duty of impartiality. Good first stop for any UK story.

  • AP NewsCENTREFREE

    Associated Press. A news agency that other papers buy stories from, so it sticks to facts.

  • ReutersCENTREFREE SIGN-UP

    The other big global wire. Strong on world events, business and markets.

  • Sky NewsCENTREFREE

    Ofcom-regulated broadcaster, so held to impartiality rules like the BBC.

Then compare: the same story, different angles

Read one from each side on the same topic. If they agree on the facts and only argue about what to do, the facts are probably solid.

  • The GuardianLEANS LEFTFREE

    Serious journalism with a centre-left view. No paywall, asks for donations.

  • The IndependentLEANS LEFTFREE

    Liberal-leaning, broad coverage, mostly free to read.

  • CapXLEANS RIGHTFREE

    Free-market, centre-right commentary from the Centre for Policy Studies. Thoughtful rather than shouty.

  • The SpectatorLEANS RIGHTSOME FREE

    Long-running conservative weekly. A few articles free each month after a free sign-up.

  • UnHerdMIXEDSOME FREE

    Deliberately contrarian essays from left and right. Good for questioning the consensus.

Tools that put sides next to each other

These do the comparing for you, which makes it a habit rather than a chore.

  • Ground NewsALL SIDESFREE TIER

    Groups every outlet covering a story and shows how left, centre and right reported it. Has a phone app.

  • AllSidesALL SIDESFREE

    Puts a left, centre and right headline on the same story side by side. US-focused but very clear.

  • TangleALL SIDESFREE TIER

    Daily newsletter: one big story, what the left says, what the right says, then the writer's own view clearly labelled.

  • The Rest Is PoliticsLEFT + RIGHTFREE

    Podcast with a former Labour adviser and a former Conservative minister disagreeing politely. Find it in any podcast app.

Checking a claim

When something online sounds alarming, look it up here before believing or sharing it.

  • Full FactINDEPENDENTFREE

    The UK's independent fact-checking charity. Searchable by topic.

  • BBC VerifyCENTREFREE

    Checks viral videos, images and claims, and shows how they did it.

  • More or LessCENTREFREE

    Radio 4 programme that takes the numbers in the news apart. 25 minutes, genuinely fun.

  • The ConversationACADEMICSFREE

    Articles written by university researchers about their own field, edited for normal readers.

Money and the world, without the hype

The antidote to "this coin will 100x" videos and "crash is coming" threads: slow, evidence-based sources measured in years, not hours.

  • MonevatorEVIDENCE-BASEDFREE

    Long-running UK investing blog. Patient, low-cost, long-term thinking. Start with their "beginner" guides.

  • Bank of England explainersOFFICIALFREE

    Plain-English answers on inflation, interest rates and why prices move.

  • Institute for Fiscal StudiesNON-PARTISANFREE

    Respected by every party. Tells you what a budget or policy actually means for people.

  • BBC BusinessCENTREFREE

    Calm daily market and economy news, explained for non-specialists.

  • Our World in DataDATAFREE

    Long-run charts on health, poverty, energy and more. Many things that feel like they're getting worse are getting better over decades.

  • GapminderDATAFREE

    From the late Hans Rosling, author of Factfulness. Animated charts and quizzes on how the world really is.

Five questions before believing a scary money post

  • Who made this, and do they earn money if I panic or buy?
  • Does a straight news source (BBC, AP, Reuters) report the same thing?
  • What does the chart look like over 1 year, or 5, rather than this week?
  • Is it a fact, a forecast, or an opinion? Forecasts are usually wrong.
  • What would I actually do differently if it's true? Often the answer is nothing.

Live charts

Each indicator is shown at 3 months and 1 year. A drop that looks like a cliff on the left often looks like a wobble on the right. Prices are live or slightly delayed, from TradingView. Click "Full chart" to zoom out to 5 years or more.

Reading these without worrying

  • Shares and crypto move every day. A 1–2% day is normal for indices; 5–10% is a normal day for crypto.
  • The VIX is the "fear gauge". Around 12–20 is calm; above 30 means markets are nervous. It always comes back down eventually.
  • Over the long run, broad indices like the S&P 500 and FTSE have risen despite wars, crashes and pandemics. Short-term charts hide that.
  • This page is for understanding, not for trading. None of it is advice to buy or sell anything.
